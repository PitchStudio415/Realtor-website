Do this first, before you shop

  • Get pre-approved. Knowing your budget is the critical first step.
  • Set aside cash for earnest money (about 3% of price) and a possible appraisal gap, so a release never catches you short.

3

The list price is low by design (I know, it's annoying)

Homes list low on purpose to draw multiple offers. 73% sold over asking last year, and not by a few thousand. The typical home sold 25% over list, sometimes hundreds of thousands over. Asking price won't win. To recommend an offer price, we use broader market comps, specifically recent sales in the area, plus what the disclosure packet tells us about the home.

Bottom line: the highest number still loses if it misses the seller's terms on closing date or contingencies. Strategy is part of the offer.

4

Before you write an offer

  • Read the disclosure and inspection package first. I turn it into a clear read with red flags and likely repair costs, so a short inspection window is all you need.
  • Set your number from the CMA: recent comparable sales, repairs needed, and the listing team's strategy.

5

What a winning offer looks like

Buyers here are more protected than in Berkeley. You can usually keep your contingencies, just on shorter timelines.

Short inspection
Keep it, but short: 7 days is common here. Waiving is an option on the most competitive homes.
Appraisal, 14 days
Often kept. If it appraises low, you can renegotiate or cover the gap in cash.
Loan, 14 days
Often kept. Release once you are fully underwritten.
Close in 14–21 days
A clean, on-time close still makes your offer stronger.

Albany taxes & point-of-sale items
