Do this first, before you buy

  • Get fully underwritten, not just pre-approved. This is what lets you safely release the loan contingency and move fast when the right home shows up.

Homes are underpriced to attract more buyers (I know, it's annoying)

Homes here are listed low on purpose to draw multiple offers, so the list price is bait, not the real number. Last year 84% of Berkeley homes sold over asking, which is exactly why the list price is not the one that wins. I run a CMA on every home so you know its true value and where it is likely to land, and we set your number from that gap between list and sold, not the asking price. The city-wide averages are up at the top of this page.

Before you write an offer

  • Read the seller's disclosure and inspection package before you write. I turn that packet into a clear, digestible read on what matters, with red flags and likely repair costs called out, so waiving the inspection contingency is informed, not a gamble.
  • Set your number from the CMA. Your realtor helps guide it based on recent sales for comparable properties, repairs needed, and more.
How a home should be reviewed

Before you write, the disclosure package and the comps both get a full, digestible analysis through AgentPrep, not a 200-page packet left for you to decode. That won't work, even when you have the highest number but miss the seller's expectations on closing dates, contingency releases, or something else.

5

What a winning offer looks like

Waive the inspection contingency
You have already read the seller's reports, so there are no surprises to fall back on.
Waive the appraisal contingency
If it appraises low, you cover the gap in cash. While this does not happen that often, it is important to be prepared for it just in case. Know that number before you write.
Loan contingency: keep or release
Keeping it is safer; releasing it is stronger. Only release once you are fully underwritten.
Release all contingencies
The most competitive offer there is. Your earnest money, 3% of price, is on the line if you walk.
Close fast: 21 days max
14 to 17 days is ideal. A clean, quick close is often worth more to a seller than a few thousand dollars.
6

Berkeley taxes & point-of-sale items

City transfer tax approx

Berkeley charges a city transfer tax at sale, roughly 1.5% up to $1.7M and 2.5% above, plus the county's ~0.11%. Customarily the seller's cost, but offering to cover part of it can make your offer stand out.

Property tax approx

Plan for about 1.2% to 1.4% of the purchase price per year, including local assessments. We confirm the exact figure for any home before you offer.

Energy rule (BESO)

Berkeley requires an energy assessment at sale. You will see the home's energy score and recommended upgrades before you close.
